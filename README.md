## Preamble
The Norman AI Doctrine exists to guide the design of trustworthy institutional intelligence systems. It recognizes that knowledge alone is insufficient, action without knowledge is dangerous, and professional practice requires oversight, correction, and trust. The doctrine seeks to help institutions learn, improve, and responsibly incorporate artificial intelligence into professional work.

## Purpose

This doctrine defines the principles required for creating trustworthy artificial intelligence systems that operate within institutions.

The doctrine is technology independent.

It does not depend upon specific:

* models
* vendors
* databases
* frameworks
* programming languages
* deployment methods

The doctrine describes how institutions transform information into trusted action through knowledge, professional practice, oversight, correction, and trust.

Technology may change.

Institutions and the principles that enable them to learn endure.

***

# Historical Inspiration

The doctrine is inspired by lessons drawn from the evolution of institutional learning and professional practice.  

Great institutions do not succeed because they possess information. They succeed because they continuously improve how information is organized, shared, reviewed, and applied.  

The historical figures Mabel Root and Dr. Henry Plummer demonstrated that principle in medicine, and this doctrine seeks to apply the same principle to modern institutional intelligence systems. 

The concepts that follow are not presented as software architectures, products, or implementation patterns. They are presented as examples of how institutions transform information into professional practice, oversight, learning, and trust.

## The Dossier

A dossier is an organized collection of knowledge, observations, records, history, evidence, and outcomes.

A dossier preserves knowledge.

Knowledge may come from:

* records
* manuals
* lessons learned
* observations
* historical outcomes
* professional experience
* institutional memory

By itself, a dossier does not act.

***
## Mabel Root

Mabel Root helped pioneer systems that transformed scattered records into organized, discoverable, and useful institutional knowledge.

Her work demonstrated that knowledge alone is not enough. Information must be located, connected, understood, and applied before it can improve outcomes.

The role represented by Mabel Root is not the possession of knowledge.

The role represented by Mabel Root is the discovery, organization, synthesis, and effective application of knowledge.

A professional must:

* locate relevant information
* connect related information
* identify patterns and precedents
* organize evidence
* communicate findings
* apply knowledge to current problems

Knowledge becomes valuable when it can be found, understood, and applied to improve outcomes.

***

## Dr. Henry Plummer

Dr. Henry Plummer did not merely collect records.

He united scattered information into a shared institutional memory and designed systems, workflows, and professional practices that allowed organizations to learn from experience.

His work demonstrated that institutions become stronger when knowledge, outcomes, and professional experience are systematically reviewed and used to improve future practice.

The role represented by Dr. Plummer is not merely oversight.

The role represented by Dr. Plummer is stewardship, governance, and continuous improvement.

Oversight provides:

* accountability
* governance
* review
* correction
* improvement

Without oversight, knowledge and practice eventually drift from reality.

Without continuous improvement, institutions stop learning.

The enduring lesson of Dr. Plummer's work is that every outcome should become an opportunity to improve the system that produced it.

# First Principle

## Knowledge Without Action Is A Library

Knowledge alone does not solve problems.

Documentation alone does not solve problems.

Records alone do not solve problems.

Knowledge must eventually be applied.

***

# Second Principle

## Action Without Knowledge Is Guessing

Recommendations without evidence are opinions.

Actions without understanding are risks.

Systems that operate without relevant knowledge are guessing, regardless of confidence.

***

# Third Principle

## Knowledge And Action Together Create Professional Practice

Professional practice emerges when knowledge is consistently applied through established methods.

Competence requires both:

```text
Knowledge
+
Action
```

Neither is sufficient alone.

***

# Fourth Principle

## Professional Practice Without Oversight Creates Chaos

Every profession requires review.

Every institution requires governance.

Every system capable of action requires oversight.

Professional practice without oversight eventually diverges from reality.

***

# Fifth Principle

## Professional Practice With Oversight Creates Trust

Trust is not granted.

Trust is earned.

Trust is maintained through:

* review
* correction
* validation
* accountability

The purpose of oversight is not punishment.

The purpose of oversight is improvement.

Trustworthy systems are not systems that never make mistakes.

Trustworthy systems are systems that surface mistakes, communicate them, discuss them, and participate in their correction.
***

# Sixth Principle

## Knowledge and Intelligence Must Remain Correctable

Any recommendation must be open to challenge.

Any stored knowledge must be capable of revision or retraction.

Any process or outcome must remain subject to correction.

A system must be capable of alerting about possible mistakes.

A system must be capable of correction after mistakes are made.

***

# Seventh Principle

## Institutional Knowledge Belongs To The Institution

Knowledge must not become dependent upon:

* a single person
* a single model
* a single vendor
* a single technology
* a single platform

Institutional knowledge must remain:

* discoverable
* reviewable
* transferable
* durable

***

# Eighth Principle

## Every Action Creates An Opportunity To Learn

Actions create results.

Results describe what happened.

Results reviewed and evaluated become outcomes.

Outcomes determine whether an action was successful, unsuccessful, or inconclusive.

Outcomes become information when they are organized and recorded.

Information becomes knowledge through review and understanding.

Knowledge becomes wisdom when it is repeatedly validated through successful professional practice.

Success creates knowledge of what to do.

Failure creates knowledge of what not to do.

An institution that learns becomes stronger.

An institution that forgets repeats mistakes.

***

# Ninth Principle

## Artificial Intelligence Is A Coworker, Not An Authority

Artificial intelligence should assist professional work.

Artificial intelligence should not become the final authority.

Responsibility remains with the institution.

Authority remains with the institution.

Accountability remains with the institution.

***

# Tenth Principle

## The Goal Is Earned Trust

Artificial intelligence must be onboarded, not merely deployed.

Artificial intelligence must listen, adapt, and internalize guiding intent.

Artificial intelligence must communicate transparently and seek assistance and clarification when unsure.

Supervisors and peers provide ongoing guidance, mentorship, and validation of operational alignment.

Competence is earned through interaction.

Trust is bestowed through demonstrated excellence.

***

# The Norman Learning Cycle

```text
Knowledge
    ↓

Action
    ↓

Outcome
    ↓

Review
    ↓

Correction
    ↓

Trust
    ↓

Knowledge
```

The cycle never ends.

The institution continues to learn.

# Commentary and Interpretation

The commentary and interpretation section exists to clarify intent without modifying the core doctrine.

The principles describe enduring truths.

The commentaries explain how those truths should be interpreted when designing modern socio-technical systems that include software, artificial intelligence, workflows, and human institutions.

***

## Commentary 1: Meaning of Action

> **Note on "Action":**
>
> Within the Norman AI Doctrine, *Action* is used in its broadest institutional sense—any application of knowledge toward an outcome.
>
> This includes:
>
> * research
> * analysis
> * reasoning
> * recommendation
> * planning
> * execution
> * review
> * correction
>
> The doctrine intentionally treats these activities as related forms of action because they all represent the transformation of knowledge into practice.
>
> The operational distinction between recommendation (advisory) and direct execution (autonomous) is an implementation and risk-management concern governed through oversight, review, and institutional controls.
>
> The level of oversight required should increase with the authority and potential impact of the action being taken.
>
> The doctrine therefore concerns itself with the relationship between knowledge and action, while system architectures, governance models, and operational policies determine the permissions, approvals, and controls associated with specific forms of action.

***

## Commentary 2: Communication and Institutional Learning

> The Norman AI Doctrine assumes that institutions learn through communication.
>
> Knowledge must be communicated to be applied.
>
> Actions must be communicated to be understood.
>
> Recommendations must be communicated to be reviewed.
>
> Mistakes must be communicated to be corrected.
>
> Corrections must be communicated to become institutional knowledge.
>
> Trust is strengthened when communication is open, honest, timely, and constructive.
>
> Artificial intelligence systems should not merely provide answers.
>
> They should be capable of:
>
> * explaining reasoning
> * communicating uncertainty
> * reporting possible mistakes
> * identifying conflicting information
> * documenting outcomes
> * participating in review and correction processes
>
> Artificial intelligence should function as a professional coworker within an institutional learning process, not as an unquestionable authority.
>
> The doctrine therefore treats communication as a foundational institutional habit that enables knowledge, action, oversight, correction, and trust to function together as a continuous learning cycle.

# One-Sentence Summary

> **Knowledge without action is a library. Action without knowledge is guessing. Knowledge and action together create professional practice. Professional practice without oversight creates chaos. Professional practice with oversight creates trust.**

# Executive Summary

The Norman AI Doctrine is not a software architecture.

It is not a product specification.

It is not a governance framework.

It is a philosophy for building trustworthy institutional intelligence systems.

Its central belief is that trustworthy intelligence emerges through the continual interaction of:

```text
Knowledge
+
Action
+
Oversight
+
Correction
+
Trust
```

Artificial intelligence, documentation systems, workflows, and human institutions all participate in this cycle.

The objective is not perfect automation.

The objective is competent, trustworthy, continuously improving professional practice.
